# CASTLE v26.9.28 — Board Working-Backwards Press Release

## The board can govern a company at machine speed without surrendering control

**September 28, 2026**

CASTLE gives large-company boards a new operating capability: define the outcomes the corporation may and may not produce, set the bounds of delegated authority, and receive durable evidence showing whether the corporation stayed inside those boundaries as conditions changed.

CASTLE begins from a harder assumption than ordinary control reporting:

> People can make mistakes. Software can fail. Vendors can fail. Automated systems can behave unexpectedly. Important parts of the operating environment can be compromised.

The board should not need every one of those assumptions to remain true.

Instead, CASTLE separates the corporation's ability to **think, analyze, simulate, plan, construct, and prepare** from its authority to **commit the corporation to a consequential outcome**.

The result is a company that can move faster without requiring the board to operate it.

---

## The board problem

A large board already receives more information than eight people can consume.

More dashboards do not solve the problem.

The missing loop is:

    board intent
      -> coordinated corporate behavior
      -> observed consequence
      -> evidence
      -> changed conditions
      -> adaptation
      -> only the consequential delta returns to the board

CASTLE turns board intent into an explicit corporate constitution:

- objectives;
- prohibited outcomes;
- capital-at-risk bounds;
- delegated authority;
- nondelegable decisions;
- escalation conditions;
- withdrawal conditions;
- evidence requirements.

Everything beneath that constitution may be decomposed, simulated, challenged, and replanned.

Nothing beneath it may silently expand the authority the board delegated.

# Eight directors, eight questions

CASTLE presents eight views of one admitted corporate state. These are not eight products.

## 1. Audit director

**Question:** Can we prove the corporation did what the board authorized?

CASTLE maintains lineage from mandate through campaign, commitment, consequence, and evidence. The board does not need to rely only on a statement that a control operated; material decisions can be reconstructed and replayed from evidence.

## 2. Risk director

**Question:** What can still cause material harm after our assumptions fail?

CASTLE begins with prohibited outcomes and searches backward for ways they could become reachable. A prohibited path is surfaced as evidence to be closed, not hidden behind a favorable aggregate score.

## 3. Capital-allocation director

**Question:** Where should the next unit of capital go, and how much of it remains reversible?

Campaigns carry explicit capital bounds and reversibility. CASTLE preserves option value before irreversible commitment and refuses a campaign that exceeds the board's capital-at-risk ceiling.

## 4. Resilience director

**Question:** Can the mission continue lawfully when a route, provider, or plan fails?

Failure of one route is not failure of the objective. CASTLE uses the smallest lawful recovery first: an existing policy branch, a valid suffix, a bounded repair, a bounded replan, or—only when the premise itself fails—a higher-level recompile.

## 5. Governance director

**Question:** Did the board's intent mutate while it traveled through the corporation?

The mandate is a source artifact. Strategy, campaigns, and evidence remain bound to it. Constraint weakening, authority expansion, and cross-campaign contamination are explicit refusal conditions.

## 6. Safety director

**Question:** How much can the company explore before reality changes?

Simulation, counterplanning, construction, and rehearsal are not commitment. CASTLE lets the company explore a large option space while preserving a narrow, independently governed consequence boundary.

## 7. Competitive-strategy director

**Question:** Are we choosing among today's options, or changing the situation so better options become possible?

CASTLE represents strategy as state transformation rather than a static plan. It can construct campaigns that concentrate, segment, change terrain, use indirection, alter tempo, preserve options, form coalitions, withdraw, or close an obsolete problem entirely.

## 8. Lead independent director

**Question:** What remains constitutionally ours?

The board identifies the decisions that cannot be silently delegated: material campaign selection, expansion of risk bounds, override of prohibited outcomes, or changes to the corporate constitution.

The board does not approve every machine action. It governs what cannot be delegated.

# Napoleon had Berthier

CASTLE uses a historical command-system pattern as prior art.

Napoleon could express strategic intent at a level far above the instructions required by a distributed force. Berthier's value was the disciplined translation of intent into coherent orders, dependencies, timing, coordination, and contingencies.

CASTLE uses the same separation:

    board constitution
          |
          v
    strategic doctrine
          |
          v
    Berthier-style compilation
          |
          +--> isolated campaign A
          +--> isolated campaign B
          +--> isolated campaign C
          |
          v
    falsification / counterstrategy / courts
          |
          v
    board SELECT
          |
          v
    inert strategic mandate
          |
          v
    separate consequence boundary

The compiler is not sovereign.

It may manufacture strategies. It may not grant itself authority.

# The 33 Strategies become maneuver operators

CASTLE does not encode historical strategy as quotations or instructions to imitate a particular person.

It extracts reusable transformations over corporate state.

Examples include:

- **Refuse the last war:** re-test the model when conditions change.
- **Concentrate:** move resources to a decisive point rather than distribute them evenly.
- **Segment:** create independently maneuverable units under common governing intent.
- **Change terrain:** alter the topology of the problem rather than fight on the strongest opposing ground.
- **Indirect approach:** reach the objective through a path the existing defense or competitor is not optimized for.
- **Tempo:** exploit time when speed changes the reachable state.
- **Preserve options:** delay irreversible commitment while useful lawful alternatives remain.
- **Coalition:** compose external capabilities rather than assume every capability must be owned.
- **Withdraw:** treat retreat as a designed state transition rather than an accidental failure.
- **Closure:** replace a recurring problem with machinery that makes the old problem unnecessary.

These are candidate operators, not authority.

Reality, evidence, board constraints, and falsifiers determine whether a campaign retains standing.

# Strategy must have a falsifier

Every admitted campaign answers:

> What observation would tell us to stop believing this strategy?

A campaign without an explicit falsifier is incomplete.

CASTLE therefore treats an invalidated premise as new information rather than an excuse to continue the old plan.

The response is proportional:

    anticipated uncertainty
      -> choose an existing policy branch

    local route failure
      -> reuse a valid suffix

    small plan divergence
      -> repair the affected tail

    bounded plan failure
      -> bounded full replan

    hierarchy premise failure
      -> recompile the hierarchy

    strategic premise failure
      -> return to the board constitution and strategically recompile

The board's constitution survives all six levels.

# The board—not the machine—selects the material campaign

CASTLE may generate and challenge multiple campaigns.

It may calculate their consequences.

It may show which premises each depends on.

It may eliminate campaigns that violate the constitution.

It does **not** turn an optimization score into corporate sovereignty.

For material strategy:

    machine CONSTRUCTS candidates
            !=
    board SELECTS campaign
            !=
    corporation DOES consequence

Board selection creates an inert mandate.

It does not itself create authority to perform consequential action.

That separation is deliberate.

# The board receipt

A material campaign should collapse back to a small board-readable state.

A CASTLE strategic receipt can answer:

    MANDATE
    What did we authorize?

    CAMPAIGN
    Which strategy did the board select?

    PREMISES
    Which assumptions still hold?

    FALSIFIED PREMISES
    What changed?

    CAPITAL
    How much is committed?
    How much remains reversible?

    OPTIONS
    How many meaningful alternatives remain?

    PROHIBITED OUTCOMES
    Has a tested path become reachable?

    AUTHORITY
    Did anything attempt to expand delegated authority?

    EXCEPTIONS
    What material exceptions exist?

    NEXT BOARD DECISION
    What future condition requires the board again?

    EVIDENCE
    Can the state be independently reconstructed?

The board sees the consequential delta rather than the operational exhaust.

# Assume important systems are compromised

CASTLE's original security thesis remains part of the product:

> Control of computation does not imply authority over consequence.

Knowledge is not authority.

Access is not authority.

A plan is not authority.

A credential is not necessarily authority for the exact consequence.

A machine that constructs a campaign cannot mint permission to commit the corporation to it.

This allows the board to authorize increasingly capable automation without treating increasing capability as increasing sovereignty.

# The first purchase

CASTLE begins with a small number of consequences the board would most hate explaining after the fact.

Examples:

1. unauthorized movement of material capital;
2. unauthorized destruction or release of critical information;
3. unsafe physical or industrial actuation;
4. an unapproved material production or infrastructure change;
5. autonomous action outside delegated corporate authority.

The board defines the boundary.

CASTLE converts it into a continuously challenged operating constraint.

Expansion happens consequence by consequence, not through a mandatory enterprise-wide replacement program.

---

# The board-level promise

CASTLE does not ask eight directors to become operators.

It does not ask them to understand every system.

It does not ask them to approve every machine decision.

It gives them a smaller governing surface:

> **Define the corporation's constitution. Select the material strategy when human sovereignty is required. Receive evidence when the constitution, strategy, or world materially changes.**

Everything else should increasingly become machinery.
